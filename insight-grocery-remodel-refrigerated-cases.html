What Happens When a Grocery Remodel Reaches the Refrigerated Cases?

December 1, 2026 · RJB Contracting

Replacing a refrigerated case can look like a simple equipment swap. In an operating grocery store, that case connects to product storage, refrigeration piping, electrical power, drainage, controls, floors, and the work happening in neighboring departments. A change in one part of that sequence can affect several teams and the store's ability to keep serving customers.

The key is to plan the case work as part of the larger remodel. Here is what the team needs to coordinate before, during, and after the replacement.

Start With the Existing Conditions

Before the schedule is set, the team needs to document the cases and the systems serving them. Case dimensions, connection points, available electrical capacity, drainage routes, floor conditions, and access for removal and delivery all shape the plan. Drawings are a starting point. Field verification matters in stores that have been repaired or remodeled over time.

On the Hannaford store remodel, RJB coordinated building systems and finishes alongside refrigeration piping and controls. That breadth is a useful reminder: a case change can reach well beyond the display itself.

Protect the Product and Plan the Shutdown

Store operations and the refrigeration contractor should establish when a case can be emptied, where the product will go, who monitors it, and when the affected system can be shut down. Temporary cold storage, backup capacity, vendor access, and the length of a tie-in window can all affect the construction sequence. The plan must follow the retailer's food safety procedures and applicable local requirements.

The FDA Food Code provides a model for retail food safety, including temperature control. The store team should decide how its specific products will be handled during the changeover. The construction team should coordinate its work around that plan.

Sequence the Trades Around the Case

Removing a case may expose flooring, walls, or utility conditions that can't be seen in advance. Plumbing and drainage work may need to happen before new finishes. Electricians may need to relocate circuits or coordinate with controls and alarms. Refrigeration specialists handle the system connections, testing, and startup. Deliveries and equipment movement must be arranged without blocking customers, associates, exits, or other trades.

RJB's Weis Markets remodel work spans several grocery departments and trades. When case work sits inside a broader department refresh, the schedule has to account for the surrounding finishes and fixtures as well as the refrigeration system.

Handle Refrigerants With the Right Specialists

If an existing system is opened or retired, refrigerant handling is a distinct part of the scope. The EPA's Section 608 guidance explains technician certification requirements for work that could release refrigerant. Its safe disposal guidance addresses recovery when retail food refrigeration equipment is dismantled on site. The refrigeration contractor should define the required steps before demolition begins.

Test the System Before the Department Returns to Service

A newly installed case isn't ready for product just because it's in place. The refrigeration team needs time for startup, controls verification, and performance checks, while the store team confirms its own requirements for stocking and operation. The project schedule should also allow for final cleaning, access checks, punch work, and any required inspections or approvals.

The handoff is clearest when construction, refrigeration, and store operations agree in advance on who signs off on each step. That keeps a technical issue or unfinished adjacent work from surfacing just as the department is set to reopen.

A Grocery Remodel Is a Coordinated Store Operation

Refrigerated case work depends on a shared plan for product, systems, people, and space. Careful site documentation and a workable shutdown sequence help the team complete the renovation while the rest of the store keeps moving. RJB plans grocery remodels in active stores around those operational realities, from department work to larger renovations.
